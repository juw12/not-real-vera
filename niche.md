# Niche research: which AI-avatar channel is worth making

Date: 2026-11-14. Method: live web searches, read as search summaries (the source pages were not opened one by one), then a weighted scoring matrix. Scores in the matrix are my own 1-5 judgement, so treat them as estimates, not measurements.

## The short answer

**Scam and deepfake defence, taught by a host who openly says she is an AI.**

Working name: **Not Real Vera** (`@notrealvera`). Tagline: *"I'm not real. The scams are."*

The usual weakness of an AI avatar (people do not trust a fake person) becomes the reason to watch: an AI shows you how an AI would trick you, then gives one 10-second check that stops it.

## Why this gap exists

1. **Demand is daily and personal.** People receive the scam message first and search for it second. A 40-second "is this text real?" answer fits how they look.
2. **Existing creators serve a different job.** Scambaiters entertain for 20-60 minutes. Banks and agencies publish accurate advice that few people watch. Short, repeatable, shareable "check" videos sit between the two.
3. **The avatar is the proof, not the weak point.** Viewers punish avatars that pretend to be human or have no expertise. A host whose premise is "I am fake, here is how fakes work" removes the pretence and gives the format a reason to exist.
4. **It is shareable upward.** People forward scam warnings to parents. Shares and saves are the signals short-form platforms reward.

## What "beating the market" would actually mean

Nobody can promise a channel will win. What can be tested in 30 days:

| Measure (first 20 episodes) | Pass | Meaning if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Median average-view percentage on Shorts | 70% or more | Hook or pacing is weak: rewrite the first 3 seconds |
| Shares plus saves per 1,000 views | 10 or more | The check is not worth forwarding: make it more concrete |
| Viewer-submitted scam messages | 5 or more | "Reply or delete?" has no pull: drop or rework that pillar |
| Comments calling it "AI slop" | under 5% of comments | The disclosure framing is not landing: change the open |

These thresholds are my own targets, not industry benchmarks. If two or more fail after 20 episodes, pivot the format before making more.

## Honest caveats

- The avatar currently is a still portrait with motion, not a lip-synced talking head. No free lip-sync tool has been proven on this PC (no dedicated GPU).
- Platform rules on AI content change. YouTube's July 2025 policy targets mass-produced, repetitive content; the defence here is that each episode covers a different, sourced scam with its own check.
- Monetisation ideas (below, in the bible) are hypotheses.
